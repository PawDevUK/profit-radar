# Chinese Cars Import to Europe — Market Report

**Date:** May 2026  
**Purpose:** Feasibility and opportunity analysis for a Chinese car import marketplace targeting European buyers.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

China is now the world's largest car exporter. In 2024 it exported over 5.8 million vehicles globally, with Europe as a key target. A significant price gap exists between Chinese domestic prices and European market equivalents — particularly for EVs — creating arbitrage and resale opportunities. However, EU tariff escalation since late 2024 has compressed margins on EVs and increased compliance complexity.

**Key opportunity**: ICE vehicles and hybrids from China still carry only the standard 6.5% import duty and offer strong price/specification value versus comparable European models.

## 4. Regulatory Requirements

### 4.1 Type Approval

To legally sell a vehicle in the EU it must have one of:

| Approval Type | Description | Cost | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WVTA** (Whole Vehicle Type Approval) | EU-wide approval, granted by a member state authority | €50,000–200,000+ | 6–24 months — done by manufacturer |
| **CoC** (Certificate of Conformity) | Issued by manufacturer if WVTA exists; needed per vehicle | €50–300/vehicle | Immediate if WVTA held |
| **IVA** (Individual Vehicle Approval) | Single vehicle approval, no WVTA needed | €500–2,500/vehicle | 2–8 weeks |
| **Small Series Type Approval** | For low-volume importers (<250 units/year) | Lower than WVTA | Varies |

> **Practical note:** Most major Chinese brands (BYD, MG, NIO, Xpeng, Ora, Lynk & Co, Polestar) already hold EU WVTA. For these, you only need the CoC per vehicle. Smaller or newer brands may require IVA per unit.

### 4.2 Emissions Standards

- All new cars must comply with **Euro 6d** (petrol/diesel).
- EVs have no direct emissions standard issue but must meet EU safety regulations (ECE/UNECE).
- From 2035: only zero-emission new cars can be sold in the EU (affects ICE import strategy long-term).

### 4.3 Required Documents Per Shipment

- **Bill of Lading** (shipping contract)
- **Commercial Invoice** (for customs valuation)
- **Packing List**
- **Certificate of Origin** (Form A / EUR.1 — may reduce duty under trade agreements)
- **EUR.1 Movement Certificate** (if origin qualifies for preference — currently China does not have EU FTA, so standard rates apply)
- **Certificate of Conformity (CoC)** or IVA certificate
- **Technical data sheet / homologation documents**
- **REACH compliance declaration** (chemical regulations)

### 4.4 Country-Specific Rules

| Country | Notable Rules |
|---|---|
| **Germany** | Strict TÜV inspection for IVA; Bremerhaven is main RoRo port |
| **Poland** | Excise tax on cars >2000cc engine or EVs (3.1% of customs value for EVs); vehicle registration requires Polish translation of CoC |
| **Netherlands** | BPM tax (purchase tax) on top of VAT — significant for ICE; EVs exempt until 2026 |
| **France** | Bonus/malus eco-score system affects pricing; malus for heavy/high-emission vehicles |
| **UK** (post-Brexit) | Separate 6.5% tariff; own type approval (DVLA); rules of origin under UK-EU TCA affect EV battery content |

## 8. Recommended Import Strategy

1. **Start with ICE/hybrid models** from brands with existing EU WVTA (Chery/OMODA, Changan, Haval) — lowest tariff, fastest approval, best margin.
2. **Target mid-spec trim levels** — highest volume segment in Europe, easiest to price competitively.
3. **Use RoRo shipping** via Zeebrugge or Bremerhaven for cost efficiency; container only for premium/rare models.
4. **Partner with a customs broker** specialising in automotive (e.g., BLG Automotive, GEFCO) — they handle CoC verification, TARIC classification, and duty calculation.
5. **Establish a warranty/service agreement** with a local garage network before listing — EU buyers expect minimum 2-year warranty.
6. **Priority markets**: Germany (volume), Netherlands (EV-friendly), Poland (price-sensitive, strong demand for value SUVs), Czech Republic (manufacturing hub, sophisticated buyers).
